# Data sources and scope of reuse

## Figure data in v30

The camera crops and calibration masters, tabulated ranges, factor shares, sampled image speeds and anonymized FY-1C analog magnitude differences are the authors’ simulation outputs. Catalog elements supply the dynamics of the virtual fragments. Size uses individual radar cross sections where available and an analog distribution otherwise. The simulations assign surfaces, shapes and spin. The public extracts use grouped trial labels or retain the model quantities needed to draw each example.

Figure 2 uses response-weighted numerical reductions of the Steckel coupon measurements credited below. Figure 12 uses the authors’ reduced TESS pixels within the published mask, the modeled Galactic field and the 57 grouped measurements. The original calibrated images are identified by the [MAST product manifest](data/tess/site2_ffi.csv). Figure S12 supplies band-integrated rates at sampled locations in two detector footprints, local SFD intensity samples and geometry. The source atlases and catalogs are external. [Figure inputs and calculations](paper/README.md).

## Model inputs and attribution

The lunar golden includes the authors' selected no-penumbra geometry and numerical readings of the unadjusted lunar spectrum in Kieffer & Stone (2005, Fig. 8) and of the simulated deployable baffle in Liu et al. (2021, Fig. 15). The two curves are comparison data with their original extraction notes. [Lunar input provenance](data/goldens/lunar/README.md).

This repository accompanies manuscript A1 v30. The scientific source is the paper's retained fragment model, diffuse sky model, calibrated TESS reduction and supplement. The inputs below retain their own origins and terms. The authors have not selected a software license. The manufacturer response curves are attributed below, and their onward redistribution terms remain unconfirmed.

The two dated sightlines and the hypothetical July lunar position are geometry inputs. The SNR grids, calibration configuration, expectation stamps, initial rotations and tabulated comparison values are the paper authors' simulation outputs. The 57-unit result refers to selected TESS total-background measurements. It does not calibrate the fragment BRDF, individual sky components, E5/E12 responses or lunar baffle.

The native-moment CSVs used for Fig. 11 (also used by historical v27 r8 Fig. 3.6) retain the scene-mean sky rates and per-stamp charge moments from the authors' simulation. Scene and object labels preserve grouping. A binary apogee class retains the original catalogue selection at 2,000 km. Full orbit records and catalogue ephemerides are outside the extract. [Scene rates, moments, and selection](data/figure36/README.md).

The historical TESS floor measurements come from the authors' MAST cutout analysis. Each row identifies the original product and retrieval request. The Blender pixels are the authors' linear Cycles renders of specified geometries and shaders. The operational estimator is extracted from Model E's 17 October 2026 source, and the repeated camera-noise experiments come from supplement S8. The angular position covariance example is the Model F shared-state HCW Monte Carlo. The three-read flux covariance experiment was added for this release. Figure 5(a), also Fig. S9(a), uses the authors' simulated target, raw DN and calibrated electrons for the first weak-sky E12 frame. The virtual sCMOS dark rate assumes the same dark-current density as the CMV4000. Starting from 125 e⁻/pixel/s at a die temperature of 25 °C and a doubling per 6.5 °C, the model gives 2.99212 e⁻/pixel/s at −10 °C for a 5.5 µm pixel. Multiplication by (2.8/5.5)² gives 0.77548 e⁻/pixel/s for the virtual sensor. The optical comparison independently propagates the three solar-weighted spectral bins in POPPY 1.1.2 and uses the paper's pixel-integrated PSF as the reference.
