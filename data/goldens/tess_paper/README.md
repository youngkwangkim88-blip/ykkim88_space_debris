# TESS common-window physical replay

Run `python gold_tess_paper.py` from the package root. This is the selected 57-unit comparison used by A1 v27. Its physical replay starts from the admitted quiet epochs, sky directions, native pixel solid angles, 100 μm dust-map samples, fitted image backgrounds (B) and unresolved-star estimates (U). The selected epochs and the image fits are held inputs here. A 25 × 25 pixel-level example is available through `tess.py`.

## Physical reconstruction

For each of 250 windows the STScI/Leinert model (Z2) averages the full list of selected times. ZodiSURF scattering (Z0) uses the original evenly spaced subset of at most 50 times per window, a geocentric observer and the 0.30–1.10 μm wavelength nodes. The supported samples have solar elongation above 80 degrees. Adding the band integral of the isotropic residual gives Z1. Each model also includes the same unresolved stars and the dust-map DGL. The DGL law caps the intensity at 25 MJy/sr and blends the north and south spectra over Galactic latitudes of ±25°. A single TESS Vega scale of 1.021408688828167 multiplies each complete window model.

The runner checks the recalculated Z2, Z0, isotropic and DGL rates against the archived window rates within 1e-7 relative error. Within each visit it takes the median of the window ratios, then takes a median over visits for each unit. It checks all three observed/model ratios for every unit. The medians across 57 units are 0.975444 (Z2), 1.201333 (Z0) and 1.091006 (Z1).

The paper's total-sky RMS uses the opposite ratio. Form (Z1 total model / observed B) − 1 in each window, take the medians across windows then visits, and take the root mean square across the 57 equally weighted units. The replay checks those individual errors before computing the approximately 9.81% result. This is a comparison of total background in this selected sample. The 156,075 entries count cadences per window. A cadence can recur across windows observing different fields.

## Data and provenance

- The committed physical oracle is `wi/WI-PHYSICS-OBSERVATION-MODEL/work/tests/test_tess_common_57_paper.py` in the upstream AI_SBSSA_v2 snapshot `c9f8989646c4e69755618cb3c187b190613e60f6`. The inputs originate in the L16 2026-10-14 observations used in A1 v24 and retained through v27. The original `units_l16.json` SHA-256 is `c9aa62001aaa76e8acebf3953f15193a928133832e549d52c886403583e09147`.
- `geometry.csv` selects seven columns from the committed `work/tests/data/tess_common_windows.csv` (Git blob `aabb1f934d680fa8eb42bc8fe5c5b9ceced5ad3f`). The window key joins the package's existing `data/tess/common_windows.csv` in the original order. That table supplies B and U and independent expected component rates. The FFI tile directions and pixel areas come from their local WCS. The 100 μm column holds the retained SFD map samples in MJy/sr.
- `tess_l16_cadences.npz` (Git blob `eed8ab0e39b32d578cb78e4f1b1d11bae5dab21f`) and `tess_common_units.csv` (Git blob `655d3734de1bb7707fc7f3ef73449cf66179d136`) are exact upstream inputs. Decode the two successive integer differences into the original float64 BTJD bits, then split at the stored lengths. Times are TDB JD − 2457000. For an FFI use the exposure midpoint. For a TESScut use the admitted TIME entries. The source MAST products and visits are listed in the existing `data/tess/common_visits.csv`.
- `band_folds.json` contains the method's integrals over the TESS response. It holds 17 solar-weighted node coefficients for the scattering calculation, one isotropic rate per steradian and two hemispheric DGL rates per steradian per unit 100 μm intensity. The effective collecting area is 0.0073984642963915905 m² before the Vega transfer. To independently regenerate those integrals supply the original response to `python gold_tess_paper.py --response-file tess-response-function-v2.0.csv`. The 1 nm TSIS-1, Chellew, and isotropic-residual inputs are in the package. [HEASARC TESS instrument response v2.0, 2020-07-30](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/tess/data/tess-response-function-v2.0.csv)
